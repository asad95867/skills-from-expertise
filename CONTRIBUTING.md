# Fixes and feedback

Every rule in this skill appeared after something went wrong during a real build. That makes
a described case far more useful than a general impression: "this part feels weak" is nearly
impossible to act on, while a situation you can walk through is.

## Tell us what didn't work

You don't need git for this. Open the **Issues** tab and click **New issue**.

What helps most:

- **what the source was**: a talk, a book, a spreadsheet, a conversation with someone;
- **which rule misfired**, or which rule was missing;
- **what came out instead** of what you expected.

If your subject area is built differently from the ones the skill was exercised on, those
reports are the most valuable of all. It has been tested across several areas, but no
methodology can cover every one, and somewhere it will turn out incomplete.

## Send a fix

Click **Fork** at the top of the page, edit your copy, and open a **Pull request**. If this
is your first time, GitHub walks through it step by step:
[creating a pull request](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/proposing-changes-to-your-work-with-pull-requests/creating-a-pull-request).

You can send a fix in either language and against either version; the other one gets updated
along with it.

## What not to send

Rules about file format: field limits, folder layout, frontmatter, progressive disclosure.
That is what the official Agent Skills specification is for, and leaving it out here
is deliberate, because this skill is about what goes inside and by what criterion.

## Questions

Ask them in **Issues** as well. There is no separate discussion board, and a question filed
as an issue is perfectly normal here.
